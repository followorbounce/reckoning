Then and now

Until the 1500s most algebra was written out in words: “the thing” for the unknown, “is equal to” spelled in full. The equals sign appeared in 1557 because, as Recorde wrote, no two things can be more equal than a pair of parallel lines. Some signs lost their contest: Harriot’s 1631 “<” for “less than” beat Hérigone’s use of the same mark for an angle three years later, and the angle sign was reshaped. The IAU style manual of 1989 still allows × only between numbers, not in symbolic formulae, where a dot is preferred.
