Things to try

  • Put two converging lenses in a row, a short focal length behind a longer one, about the sum of their focal lengths apart. That is a Keplerian telescope: a parallel beam goes in and a narrower parallel beam comes out.
  • Send a single ray into a right-angled prism through its long face. It comes back out the way it came, totally reflected twice.
  • Aim a narrow beam at a glass drop and at a water drop. Compare where the light that reflects once inside comes out.
  • Put a diverging lens in a converging beam just before the focus. The focus moves back: that is a Barlow lens, or the back half of a telephoto lens.
  • Tilt a block of glass in a beam. The rays come out parallel to how they went in, only shifted sideways.
What this model leaves out

These are rays, the geometrical-optics picture: light is treated as lines that bend and split at surfaces. Diffraction and interference, which need the wave picture, are not shown. The bench is a flat cross-section, so a lens here is really a cylinder of glass seen end-on. That is exact for rays in the plane of the drawing. Air is taken as n = 1, and the glass and water indices are given relative to air, so the angles are right. Colours on screen are an approximation of the spectrum.
