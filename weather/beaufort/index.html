Beaufort scale

Francis Beaufort’s 1805 scale judged wind by its effect on a ship’s sails. Today it is read from the sea itself, and each force has a defined range of wind speed. Move the slider: the sea below is drawn to scale beside a 10-metre boat.

A formula behind the numbers

The speed ranges follow v ≈ 0.836 B3/2 m/s, where B is the force: force 6 gives 12.3 m/s, inside its 10.8–13.8 range. The build checks that every force’s mid-speed falls inside its own range. It also found that the knot and m/s columns are two separately rounded tables: at forces 4 to 6 their boundaries differ by up to half a metre per second (16.5 knots is 8.5 m/s, but the m/s table changes force at 8.0).

Then and now

Older tables named forces 7 to 10 moderate gale, fresh gale, strong gale and whole gale. Speeds were attached to the numbers only in the twentieth century; Beaufort’s own scale described the canvas a man-of-war could carry.
