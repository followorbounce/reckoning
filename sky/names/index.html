Names from the sky

The gods of Greece and Rome named the planets, and through them the days of the week, half the months and a dozen chemical elements. Today the International Astronomical Union keeps the custom alive with a theme for each planet’s moons.

Rome took most Greek gods by identifying them with its own (Zeus with Iuppiter, Hera with Juno); a few, like Apollo, came over with their Greek names. The Roman god of the Sun was Sol. Apollo and Artemis came to be linked with the Sun and Moon only later. For the planets themselves, see Planets and their names.

Moons: a theme for each planet

PlanetMoonsTheme
MarsPhobos, DeimosThe sons of Ares who drove his chariot: Fear and Dread. Found by Asaph Hall in 1877.
JupiterIo, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto and dozens moreLovers, favourites and descendants of Zeus. Galileo found the four large moons in 1610; their names were proposed by Simon Marius. Under the present convention the names of prograde moons end in -a and of retrograde moons in -e.
SaturnTitan, Rhea, Iapetus, Dione, Tethys, Enceladus, Mimas, Hyperion…Titans and giants, named by John Herschel in 1847; the many small outer moons are grouped by giants of Inuit, Gallic and Norse myth.
UranusTitania, Oberon, Ariel, Umbriel, Miranda…An exception: characters from Shakespeare and Alexander Pope, not myth. Titania and Oberon rule the fairies in A Midsummer Night’s Dream.
NeptuneTriton, Nereid, Proteus…Gods and spirits of the sea. Triton was found 17 days after Neptune itself, in 1846.
PlutoCharon, Nix, Hydra, Kerberos, StyxThe underworld: its ferryman, river, guard dog and monsters.

Rules and themes: Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature; Jupiter’s convention: Jovian moon names.

Dwarf planets

  • Ceres: Roman goddess of grain; patron of Sicily, where Giuseppe Piazzi found it on 1 January 1801
  • Pluto: Roman god of the underworld; a planet from 1930 to 2006
  • Eris: Greek goddess of strife: its discovery in 2005 started the debate that led the IAU to define “planet” in 2006
  • Haumea: Hawaiian goddess of childbirth; its moons are her daughters Hiʻiaka and Namaka
  • Makemake: creator god of the people of Rapa Nui (Easter Island)

The seven-day week named after the seven moving lights of the sky (Sun, Moon and the five planets seen by eye) spread through the Roman world in the first centuries AD. The Germanic peoples put their own gods in place of the Roman ones. Romance languages took the Christian “Lord’s day” (dies Dominica). Spanish and French took the Sabbath. Etymologies: OED.

September to Decemberseventh to tenth (septem, octo, novem, decem), counting from March

September to December keep the numbers of a Roman year that began in March, which is why the ninth month is called the seventh.
