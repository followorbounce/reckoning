Nouns are given with their article, because the gender comes with the word. Spellings and nautical senses checked in RAE and CNRTL. Some words have more than one equivalent at sea; the one given is the usual one in a ship’s papers.

One word, several meanings

pitch

  • Ship: the bow rising and falling as the ship rocks fore and aft
  • Code of life: the length of one full turn of the double helix: 3.4 nm for B-DNA
  • Shape: the distance between threads of a helix or screw

list

  • Ship: a steady lean caused by the ship’s own loading, as opposed to heel
  • everyday: a written series of items

knot

  • Measure: a speed: one nautical mile per hour
  • Rope: a tied rope; see the Knot Atlas
  • Topology: a closed curve in space that cannot be untangled; DNA can be knotted too

twist

  • Code of life: Tw, how many times a ribbon turns about its own axis; with writhe it adds up to the linking number
  • Rope: the lay of the strands in a rope

beam

  • Ship: the width of the ship; also a transverse girder under a deck
  • Engineering: any bar loaded across its length

sign

  • Sky & sea: one of the twelve 30° divisions of the ecliptic, now about one sign away from the constellation of the same name
  • Number: a mathematical symbol; also plus or minus

characteristic

  • Number: the integer part of a common logarithm
  • Shape: the Euler characteristic V − E + F, a topological invariant

strand

  • Code of life: one of the two chains of a DNA double helix
  • Rope: a group of yarns twisted together, three or four of which make a rope

bearing

  • Navigation: the direction of an object, measured clockwise from north
  • Engineering: a part that carries a turning shaft

draught

  • Ship: how deep a ship floats (US: draft)
  • Weather: a current of air
